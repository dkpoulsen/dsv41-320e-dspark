# Fitting DSpark on the Five-Card Node: The Memory Problem, the Payoff, and the Decision

**Answer up front.** The DSpark draft head cannot be made to fit on the current five-card PP5 deployment by shrinking, offloading, re-tuning, or re-partitioning anything: the realistic shortfall is ~7 GiB against a rank already sitting at the hardware limit. The fit must come from a new degree of freedom, and three such degrees exist — two viable with confidence, and one (320E) arithmetic-plausible only under in-budget accounting. REAP-320E on the existing cards fits on paper with zero code changes but carries the thinnest margin (~+0.70 GiB, which the ~1.6 GiB accounting wedge turns into a roughly −0.9 GiB physical margin — its first load is expected to fail, making it a cheap probe rather than a destination) and a checkpoint that must be built, at ~+1.5–2.2% extrapolated perplexity (the honest bound is the 304E ladder measurement, +2.21% [[github-libertaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-reap-expert-pruning-for-deepseek-v41-flas]]). REAP-272E buys a comfortable +7.43 GiB margin with a published checkpoint at the cost of one kernel-table case and a published +4.13% perplexity regression [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]. A sixth 64 GiB card at PP6 `7,7,7,7,7,5` gives the widest margin (+8.36 GiB worst-rank), zero accuracy cost, and a ~7× larger KV pool — but requires hardware. Gate any spend on two measurements that cost nothing: the Marlin MXFP4 expert-read rate on this node under a verification-shaped batch, and DSpark's acceptance rate on reasoning-dominated output with the shipped V4.1-Flash draft — both unmeasured anywhere, and the second precisely on the workload this node produces. The speedup is therefore genuinely two-sided: plausibly ~2.4×, plausibly a net loss, because the expert-read tax under verification is itself unmeasured and spans a range wide enough to flip the sign (Section 4). On the prior: the evidence points in two directions and must be reported as such. The only study that measured a *generic* drafter found reasoning domains near break-even; DSpark's own *trained-drafter* data shows structured reasoning is its strongest domain (5.57 accepted on math versus 3.49 on chat [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]]), and its confidence-pruning results push acceptance above 90% on all task types. The honest prior therefore leans favourable on acceptance — with the explicit caveat that the paper's numbers come from Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and Gemma4-12B targets, not this model, and the shipped draft's behaviour on V4.1-Flash reasoning traces remains the highest-value unmeasured number in the plan. And if the sixth card is bought, justify it by the KV-pool payoff rather than by an assumed speedup: the speedup case is genuinely uncertain, gated on the two unmeasured quantities above, whereas the KV-pool case survives regardless of how either measurement returns.

## 1. The Memory Budget That Must Be Satisfied

Any fit plan starts with the arithmetic of what already occupies the card that would host the draft, and on this node that arithmetic is unforgiving because the deployment is at the cliff edge before the draft is even considered. The node runs five CMP 170HX-class cards of 64 GiB each, PCIe-only with no peer-to-peer [[250503782-exploration-of-cryptocurrency-mining-specific-gpus-in-ai-applications]], serving DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash [[readmemd-deepseek-aideepseek-v41-flash-at-main]] under pipeline parallelism (PP — the model's layers are split across devices so each card holds a contiguous slice, 8 layers per rank in the current PP5 layout). The node's PCIe-only, no-P2P topology and GA100-class silicon are confirmed by local hardware inspection (`lspci`, `nvidia-smi topo -m`) [[250503782-exploration-of-cryptocurrency-mining-specific-gpus-in-ai-applications]]; the mining-GPU case-study literature [[250503782-exploration-of-cryptocurrency-mining-specific-gpus-in-ai-applications]] independently documents such cards' adoption for AI applications. Measured on this node, each 8-layer rank holds 56.71 GiB of weights and a 1.55 GiB KV pool (1,814,599 tokens — ≈0.9 KiB per token per rank, arithmetic: 1.55 GiB ÷ 1,814,599 tokens) against a 62.08 GiB budget at `gpu_memory_utilization 0.97` — slack of −0.02 GiB. **There is no headroom to steal.**

Two framings of *free memory* exist, and honesty requires presenting both because they differ by a non-trivial wedge. The in-budget framing says the last rank has −0.02 GiB of slack under vLLM's own accounting [[speculative-vllm]]. The physical framing is worse: `nvidia-smi` on the live server shows the draft-bearing last rank using 64,337 of 65,536 MiB, leaving roughly 1.2 GiB physically free, because CUDA contexts, NCCL buffers, and CUDA-graph pools live outside vLLM's memory accounting. The ~1.6 GiB difference between the two framings is that wedge. Any plan validated only against the in-budget number has not yet faced the real constraint.

What the draft needs must be counted with the same honesty. The REAP repository's component table lists the draft as three MTP layers, 7.4 GiB, in E2M1 [[github-libertaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-reap-expert-pruning-for-deepseek-v41-flas]]; local measurement of the checkpoint and an independent GGUF export (7.97 GB across 78 tensors) put the exact figure at 7.39 GiB — three multi-token-prediction (MTP) stages in MXFP4. Almost all of that is irreducible by construction: the shared embedding and the output head (129,280 vocabulary entries, read from the model config in the fork source [[readmemd-deepseek-aideepseek-v41-flash-at-main]]) are common with the target and cannot shrink without changing the target too, and the three MTP expert blocks — the only nominally compressible part — are closed to shrinking by Section 2's kernel-floor and graph-capture findings. Practically the entire head is irreducible, which is why no squeeze lever appears below. But weights are not the whole cost. The deployment documentation is explicit that the draft carries its own state: there are "43 request-owned windows: 40 target and three draft context windows", and the draft has its own graph [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]]. That CUDA graph is a full-graph capture mirroring the target's decode replay [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]], and full-graph capture keeps private memory pools [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]] — the backport's own documentation warns these pools "can cost >800 MB per GPU" [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]] and can OOM warmup at high `--gpu-memory-utilization`. Adding the draft's KV windows and graph reserve to its weights gives a realistic requirement of ≈8.2 GiB (arithmetic: 7.39 GiB weights + ~0.3 GiB draft KV windows + ~0.5 GiB graph pool).

The shortfall is therefore **~7 GiB, not the ~5.1 GiB** that a weights-only calculation implies — and a weights-only calculation is exactly the mistake that makes this problem look easier than it is. This single number closes the entire *tune-around-it* family before any measurement is run [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]. Raising utilization from 0.97 to 1.00 gains 1.97 GiB. Shrinking the KV pool from 1.55 to 1.00 GiB frees another 0.55 GiB at the cost of 35% of the context window. Best case, 2.47 GiB against a ~7 GiB requirement (arithmetic from the measured budget and pool figures above). 

One distinct lever deserves explicit rejection: KV *quantization*, as opposed to KV shrinkage [[260919969-deepseek-v41-flash-pushing-the-limits-of-kv-cache-compression]]. The pool is already fp8 in this deployment (`fp8_ds_mla` — a code-level dtype of the fork's serving path, not the paper's format: the model's own KV paper describes a different scheme, CSA2 cross-layer reuse with FP4 KV caching at 890 bytes/token [[260919969-deepseek-v41-flash-pushing-the-limits-of-kv-cache-compression]]) — and the generic 2-bit lever of KIVI [[240202750-kivi-a-tuning-free-asymmetric-2bit-quantization-for-kv-cache]] is a precision trade with, at best, ~2× on an already-compressed 1.55 GiB pool: under 0.8 GiB against a ~7 GiB gap, paid for in accuracy on every request. The gap cannot be closed by budget manipulation; it can only be closed by changing where bytes live. 

The same arithmetic kills the five-card rebalance before it starts: one layer measures 7.089 GiB, so the 9-layer rank that any rebalance must create somewhere holds 63.80 GiB of weights alone — 67.96 GiB with KV and reserve — over the 64 GiB device at every utilization setting, including 1.00, which admits at most 8.44 layers. The uneven partitions that would help the draft's rank necessarily deepen some other rank past 8 layers [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]].

One further consequence: the blocker is binding in kind, not in degree — and the code-level constraint that at first read appears to forbid the escape route turns out, on precise reading, to be the mechanism of the escape, developed in Section 3.

## 2. Why the Draft Head Cannot Simply Be Made Smaller

The natural instinct is that a 7.4 GiB draft must be squeezable. It is not, and every axis of squeeze is closed by a measurement or a structural fact on this node, not by argument.

**Offload to host is closed twice over.** The first closure is empirical. Offloading 10.94 GiB of rank-4 target experts (`--cpu-offload-gb 9`) [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]] collapsed decode to a peak of 13.8 tok/s against the corrected baseline's 40.3 tok/s — a ~2.9× decode regression over a pinned host-to-device path that measures 1.69 GB/s on this Gen2 x4 fabric with no P2P (measured on this node). Both figures are read from vLLM's own server-side telemetry (`Avg generation throughput`) rather than the benchmark harness, which makes them the more trustworthy pair. 

A note on the baseline: an earlier measurement generation reported 74 tok/s single-stream, but that figure is a benchmark artifact — the harness timed its first token on `content` deltas only, while this model streams ~595 of 600 chunks on a technical prompt in a separate `reasoning` field, inflating the rate roughly 2× by dividing all completion tokens by the short content tail. The server's own telemetry confirms the corrected figure (42.1 tok/s steady single-stream), and 40.3 tok/s peak is the baseline used for the offload comparison. Because both legs of the offload experiment come from server telemetry, the ~2.9× penalty does not depend on the harness correction at all — and even if the offload leg predated the correction, the correction could only raise its apparent rate, so the measured penalty is, if anything, understated.

The second closure is structural. Offloading the *draft's own* experts does not merely perform badly — it cannot be done. The draft is loaded by a separate `Speculator` module outside the offloader's `wrap_modules` path; a name-filtered offload matched zero parameters and the rank OOM'd at draft load. Even with plumbing, the draft's step is CUDA-graph captured — the fork's speculator module docstring (`vllm/v1/worker/gpu/spec_decode/dspark/speculator.py:22`) records CUDA graphs (FULL, mirroring DFlash) covering the whole draft step [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]] — and graph replay re-issues captured addresses, which excludes host-resident weights by construction. 

Smarter schemes from the MoE-offload literature — expert-access prefetching [[231217238-fast-inference-of-mixture-of-experts-language-models-with-offloading]], module-based caching on a single GPU [[250309716-moe-gen-high-throughput-moe-inference-on-a-single-gpu-with-module-base]], fine-grained latency–memory trading [[250205370-taming-latency-memory-trade-off-in-moe-based-llm-serving-via-fine-grai]] — do not escape either closure: their mechanism is hiding expert *load* traffic behind compute, which presumes load bandwidth far above this node's measured 1.69 GB/s pinned path and the address stability that CUDA-graph replay forbids. At single-stream decode there is neither spare compute nor bandwidth headroom to hide an ~18.6 MB/expert transfer behind, so the measured ~2.9× regression is the best case such scheduling could approach, not beat.

**Requantization is closed by kernel physics and format floor.** The `exl3_moe` GEMM path saturates at ~156–168 GB/s [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]] [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]] — about 10% of the card's ~1.6 TB/s bandwidth — and never improves with batching; an optimized rewrite bought only 1.26× and still lands at ≈17 tok/s against the current 42 (measured on this node). A requantization that exists to save memory therefore costs 2.4× the decode speed: a regression, not a rescue. And there is no precision left to spend at the top end anyway: the checkpoint's experts are already MXFP4, and the one lower-format alternative on the table, NVFP4, makes the file *bigger* (4.5 vs 4.25 bits/weight) [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]. The 2-bit EXL3 path exists but lands on the ~17 tok/s kernel just described [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]]. The asymmetry has a hardware root worth naming: MXFP4 runs on sm_80 only through dequantizing Marlin/Triton kernels — GA100 has no FP4 or FP8 tensor-core MMA — and it is the same small-GEMV dequant path that pins the ~156–168 GB/s ceiling above, not a defect of one vendor's kernel.

**Fewer MTP stages dismantles the thing being fitted [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]].** DSpark's draft is a semi-autoregressive block of five tokens produced across three trained MTP stages with a confidence head [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]] [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]]. Truncating the block size below 5 breaks the semi-autoregressive structure; shrinking it to 1 collapses DSpark back to the MTP-1 baseline the paper's gains are measured against. There is no cheaper half-measure that captures part of the gain — which means the fit decision is binary (full draft or none of the speedup), and there is no low-cost pilot configuration that de-risks the bet. For completeness against the block-diffusion alternative: no DFlash drafter exists for this checkpoint in the record, so no draft-size or acceptance comparison is measurable here — the shipped checkpoint ships only the DSpark drafter, and DFlash enters this report solely as the graph-capture precedent the fork's draft mirrors.

**Five-card rebalancing is closed by the layer arithmetic of Section 1.** No partition of 40 layers over 5 ranks produces a last stage light enough for the draft without pushing another rank to 9+ layers, and 9 layers do not fit. PP5-with-draft needs 70.34 GiB on the draft rank against 62.08.

The conclusion of the squeeze survey is clean: **no transformation of the current five-card deployment creates 7–8 GiB.** Every surviving route changes the number of cards, the size of the target, or the process topology — and those are the subjects of the next three sections.

## 3. Placement Strategies: Evidence from Published Systems

Before evaluating placements, a primer on why this draft is unusually hard to place. DSpark's draft is not a standalone model. It consumes the target's layer 37, 38, and 39 hidden states [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]] ("Target layer inputs 37, 38 and 39 contribute the mean of the four residual streams"), shares the target's embedding and its output head (129,280 vocabulary entries, per the model config in the fork source), and keeps its three KV windows inside the target's 43-window ring under a tentative-state/commit contract [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]]. A placement that severs any of those couplings does not degrade the draft — it stops existing. With that in mind, the placement search space has exactly three families: co-locate the draft on a new, small last pipeline stage; shrink the target so the draft fits on an existing stage; or run the draft as a separate process on separate hardware.

### A. The constraint reversal: the gate that looks fatal is the mechanism of the fix

The fork's code enforces that the draft's auxiliary hidden states come from the target's last layers [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]] and raises `ValueError: V4.1 PP auxiliary states must all be on the last stage` otherwise; the gate's code comment (`vllm/models/deepseek_v4_1/nvidia/model.py:418`) states the rule directly: a draft head's aux hidden states are captured from the entry stream of its target layers and are only forwarded by the last PP stage, so PP is allowed as long as every aux layer sits on that stage — the code text lives in the fork, not in the deployment-thread note. Read loosely, this sounds like a prohibition on re-partitioning. Read precisely, the gate fails only if the last stage *starts at or above layer 37* — that is, it demands a last stage of at least four layers. The constraint does the opposite of blocking the fix: it *forces the last stage to be small*, and **a small last stage is exactly what creates room for the 8.2 GiB draft**. The same constraint is what makes PP5 impossible (8 draft-hosting layers on a stage that must also carry the draft = the 70.34 GiB of Section 1). The constraint everyone feared is the constraint that solves the problem.

### B. The six-stage pipeline, field-proven in shape

The verified PP6 layout `7,7,7,7,7,5`, built from real per-layer sizes (min 6.882, max 7.042, mean 6.891 GiB — layers are near-uniform), gives: ranks 0–4 carry 48.2–49.6 GiB of weights plus KV and reserve, worst rank 53.72 GiB against the 62.08 budget (+8.36 GiB slack). The draft-bearing last stage sits at 35.65 GiB of weights plus head and draft, 48.05 GiB committed (+14.03 GiB slack). The configuration class is field-proven, not merely derived: the public Ampere support thread records four independent CMP 170HX deployments running DSpark under PP, including a 4-card rig at `VLLM_PP_LAYER_PARTITION=12,12,12,7` — an uneven partition with a light final stage, the same topology derived here [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]] — reporting a 4,991,054-token KV pool, 4.76× concurrency at 1M context [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. The details of that field record deserve enumeration because they double as a compatibility census: a 3-card PP3 + DSpark rig at fp8 KV running 262k context; a 2-card REAP-K160 rig at 20.5 tok/s single-stream at 128k; and a 4-card REAP-K160 rig reaching 20/20 on the 67k needle suite after the tie-handling fix. Every combination this report recommends — PP with a light last stage, DSpark, REAP checkpoints, this exact GPU — appears in at least one working deployment. Two scope qualifiers keep that census honest: the deployment records, and the corruption findings built on them, come from the PR thread's later comments (archived in a companion note [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]) rather than the stored issue body; and every fielded rig runs DSpark against V4/V4-Flash-0731 targets, not V4.1-Flash — so this exact GPU is proven, but this exact model-plus-draft pairing is not. Section 4 verifies the remaining structural gates in the fork source.

### C. Process disaggregation is incoherent — architecturally, not economically

The fashionable option, represented by an open RFC for disaggregated speculative decoding with a standalone draft server, proposes a speculation cache keyed by `(seq_id, k_accepted, bonus_token)` [[rfc-disaggregated-speculative-decoding-with-standalone-parallel-draft-model-issu]]. That wire protocol presupposes a drafter that can free-run on its own tokens. This draft cannot: its inputs are a `[T, 3×5120]` aux tensor cut from the target's forward pass, its output head is the target's, and its KV windows live inside the target's ring. None of that crosses the RFC's protocol. The RFC's own economics argue against it here on independent grounds — disaggregation wins only when the two phases contend for different resources and the transfer is small relative to phase time [[240109670-distserve-disaggregating-prefill-and-decoding-for-goodput-optimized-la]] [[241018038-pod-attention-unlocking-full-prefill-decode-overlap-for-faster-llm-inf]]; the RFC's own asymptotic for this is `1 + d/v` [[rfc-disaggregated-speculative-decoding-with-standalone-parallel-draft-model-issu]], and here the draft's per-block work (3 small expert GEMMs) is tiny against a 56.7 GiB-per-rank MoE target step, so `d/v ≪ 1`. The single field report of cross-node draft serving was *slower than no speculation*, its author conceding the disaggregated-MTP case's real win "isn't faster speculation — it's better memory utilization" [[rfc-disaggregated-speculative-decoding-with-standalone-parallel-draft-model-issu]] — which is precisely the sixth-card case, obtainable without the incoherence. And the RFC's maintainer asked the author directly whether disaggregated *MTP* is worth the engineering effort; the question is unanswered and the RFC has no linked PR. That unanswered question is itself a finding: the one party positioned to know does not know either. One distinction must be kept sharp: what fails is a *separate process*, not a *separate device*. Same-process cross-device placement of the existing `Speculator` module stays coherent — per-round aux payloads of ≈0.25–2 MB cost ≈0.15–1 ms at the measured 1.69 GB/s, milliseconds against a ~24 ms step — but it requires fork-side engineering of window bookkeeping and graph re-keying that no source, upstream or local, has demonstrated. It ranks below anything that runs today. Weight/KV prefetching schemes for disaggregated serving [[250108192-preserve-prefetching-model-weights-and-kv-cache-in-distributed-llm-ser]] likewise presuppose a standalone drafter whose weights are portable, which the hidden-state-coupled DSpark draft is not.

**Sharding the draft itself (TP or EP) is not a fit lever.** In principle the question is open — upstream is decoupling draft parallelism from the target (RFC #53673 [[rfc-decouple-draft-model-parallelism-from-the-target-model-pcpdcp-speculative-de]]), and this fork builds the draft with `pipeline_parallel_size=1` and `tensor_parallel_size=draft_parallel_config.tensor_parallel_size`. In practice it is closed here twice. Config: the decoupling is an upstream RFC, not wired into this fork's DSpark path, whose aux-state gate and ring-window bookkeeping assume the draft runs whole on the last rank. Economics: expert-parallel sharding of the draft's 128 routed experts over 5–6 ranks does not divide cleanly (128 is not a multiple of 5 or 6), and per-stage tensor sharding of a ~2.5 GiB-per-stage head has no payoff when the entire head fits on one rank — each sharded draft step would add all-reduce/all-to-all crossings on a no-P2P PCIe fabric against a ~24 ms step, buying room that already exists.

### D. Prune the target: closed by choice, not hardware

Pruning the target so the draft fits on the existing five cards is viable in principle and closed only by choice in practice — checkpoint availability and one kernel-table entry, never hardware. Its arithmetic and costs are laid out in Section 5.

### E. The acceptance evidence, reconciled: favourable-leaning, unresolved

Because the placement effort is only worth its cost if the draft pays, the acceptance literature belongs in this section, and it must be presented in full rather than summarized into a caution. Two lines of evidence point in opposite directions, and they reconcile precisely.

The adverse line first. The closest *generic-drafter* measurement found that across 99,768 speculative nodes in four cognitive domains, "only the chat domain consistently yields an expected accepted length exceeding 1.0 token per step," with "task type is a stronger predictor of acceptance than tree depth" [[260414682-acceptance-dynamics-across-cognitive-domains-in-speculative-decoding]]. Since this node's output is reasoning-dominated (measured locally: ~595 of 600 streamed chunks on a technical prompt were reasoning content), that result, read alone, is bad news.

The favourable line is DSpark's own per-domain data, and it is stronger than a vague mechanism argument. DSpark inherits the multi-token-prediction lineage [[240419737-better-faster-large-language-models-via-multi-token-prediction]], and its acceptance rule rests on the speculative-sampling guarantee — accepted tokens are drawn losslessly from the target's distribution [[230201318-accelerating-large-language-model-decoding-with-speculative-sampling]] — so the confidence-pruning figures below can be read as accuracy-neutral by construction. On structured tasks the paper reports accepted lengths of "5.57 on math and 5.12 on code for Qwen3-4B" against 3.49 on open-ended chat [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]] — reasoning is the drafter's *strongest* regime, the exact inverse of the generic-drafter pattern. The confidence-pruning ablation reinforces the point from a second axis: before pruning, raw acceptance ran 45.7% on chat versus 76.9% on math and 67.6% on code; under pruning, chat rose to 95.7% while math and code reached 92.5% and 92.0% — "structured tasks (Math and Code) experience milder pruning and retain more draft tokens" [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]]. In other words, the domains where a generic drafter collapses are the domains where a trained semi-autoregressive drafter retains the most of its own proposals.

The two findings are reconcilable rather than contradictory, and the reconciliation matters for how much weight each carries here. The adverse study measured a TinyLlama-1.1B tree drafter [[260414682-acceptance-dynamics-across-cognitive-domains-in-speculative-decoding]] against Llama-2-7B: a drafter far too small and architecturally generic to track its target's reasoning register, which is precisely the failure mode that acceptance-on-reasoning exposes. DSpark's block is trained on the target family and its semi-autoregressive structure exists specifically to counter the intra-block suffix decay that guts acceptance on high-entropy output. So the correct synthesis is: one generic-drafter study cautions that *an untrained drafter* fails on reasoning; DSpark's own trained-drafter data favours reasoning; and what remains genuinely unmeasured is the specific shipped V4.1-Flash draft on this model's reasoning traces. Two scope caveats keep the favourable line honest: the paper's numbers are on Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and Gemma4-12B targets, not DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and its measured configuration is not this deployment. The prior therefore leans favourable, but with the interim calibration carried rather than flattened: the fit arithmetic behind Sections 1–2 rests on high-confidence (>85%), line-cited fork-source reads, while this payoff prior is the weakest link in the record — held at only medium confidence (~60%) by the analysis that produced it, because every accepted-length number behind it is offline and on other model families. The tiebreak remains a measurement, and Section 6 keeps that gate exactly where it was — before any spend.

## 4. The Dedicated Draft Stage: Arithmetic and Communication Cost

The PP6 route survives every structural gate in the fork source [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]], each verified by direct code reading — structurally, that is: by code reading and arithmetic, not by execution. Three draft-side questions were left open by that reading and are carried as gates, not passed; they are listed after the verified gates below. This matters because the difference between a derived layout and a verified one is exactly the set of places a partition change silently breaks.

**Aux gate.** The assertion requires `last_start ≤ 36` for aux layers {37, 38, 39} [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]]. The layout `7,7,7,7,7,5` starts its last stage at layer 35 — satisfied with one layer of margin. A 4-layer last stage starting at 36 also passes; only last stages of ≤3 layers fail, and no memory-feasible partition produces one. The fork's default PP6 partition `[6,7,7,7,7,6]` also satisfies the gate — its last stage starts at layer 34, two layers of margin, with no environment variable needed. This report pins `7,7,7,7,7,5`, which lightens the draft rank one layer further by trading a layer onto ranks 1–4; both layouts pass the gate, and the pinned choice buys extra draft-rank headroom at a small cost on the middle ranks (arithmetic from the per-layer figures of Section 3).

**The shadow-source planner is general, not hardcoded.** `_plan_shadow_sources` is a pure function of `get_pp_indices(n_layers, rank, pp_size)` [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]] — it re-derives the cross-rank replicas of KV-source and candidate-source layers for any partition. Under PP6 it yields shadows on ranks 1:{2}, 3:{20}, 4:{20}, 5:{20} — 4 shadow modules versus PP5's 3, costing ~0.125 GiB total, with the draft-bearing rank carrying one shadow in both layouts. The cross-boundary shadow pattern PP6 produces is the same pattern already in production at PP5, where source layer 20 sits on rank 2 while its consumers sit on rank 4. No code path asserts on boundary placement.

**Draft-token sync across PP ranks is implemented.** `pp_utils.py` carries `broadcast_drafts`, whose docstring (`vllm/v1/worker/gpu/pp_utils.py:166`) records that draft proposals are broadcast so non-last ranks can embed real token ids, a `receive()` allocating `[num_reqs, max_sample_len]`, and a pipeline-delay-depth queue with free-detection generation counters [[mrv2bugfix-sync-speculative-draft-tokens-across-pp-ranks-by-lostfox11-pull-reque]], wired at `model_runner.py:2092` (fork source: `vllm/v1/worker/gpu/pp_utils.py`, called from `vllm/v1/worker/gpu/model_runner.py`). The correctness mechanism for PP-plus-speculation stacks is present in this fork, including the stale-draft fix.

**Load-time peak is *lower* at six stages** — a non-obvious result with operational weight. The staged Marlin repack holds a ~13 GiB transient per layer [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]] [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]], so the memory peak lands at the tail of load: the PP5 8-layer rank peaks at 7×6.891+13 = 61.24 GiB, a 2.76 GiB margin — which is exactly why a 20 MB allocation shortfall was previously fatal on this node. The PP6 7-layer rank peaks at 54.35 GiB (8.65 GiB margin); the PP6 last stage at 40.56 GiB. Re-partitioning does not merely create steady-state room; it removes the most fragile moment of the boot sequence [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]]. At 61.24 GiB peak against a 62.08 GiB budget, the status quo is *closer to the cliff at load* than the six-stage alternative.

**Host RAM is untouched.** The Engram tables sit on layers 1 and 14 [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]], so their pinned copies move from ranks {0,1} to {0,2} — but the total stays 188.8 GiB either way (arithmetic: two pinned table copies of ≈94.4 GiB each, one per Engram layer at layers 1 and 14, from `engram_layer_ids [1,14]` and the measured table size).

**Communication cost is one serial PCIe hop per decode step, and it is unmeasured** [[specpp-support-mtp-speculative-decoding-under-pipeline-parallelism-pp1-by-atassi]]. Six-rank NCCL broadcast volume rises; two offsets partially compensate: aux tensors never leave the last rank (they did not broadcast at PP5 either), and per-step comm shrinks relative to any design that moves aux across ranks. The unmeasured quantity — six-hop step latency on this no-P2P fabric — bounds the *payoff*, not the *fit*. The reassuring PP3/PP4 results on similar fabric in the public thread are not equivalent measurements, and treating them as such would be the report's one dishonest move. The latency question is a first-boot profiling item, and Section 6 makes it a gate before production; the working prior is a ~20% decode-latency rise before spec-decode amortizes it.

**Three draft-side questions were left open, not passed** [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]]. First, whether the draft device needs the target's *embedding* (not just the shared output head) replicated for the noise-token/Markov-rank input path, and at what memory cost on the draft rank. Second, draft-device access to the staged Engram compressor histories: the tables sit on layers 1 and 14 — ranks 0 and 2 at PP6 — while the draft runs on the last rank, and no source demonstrates the draft reading across that distance. Third, whether the hc-widened residual path (`hc_mult=4`) engages for V4.1, which would change the per-round draft payload by ~2.7×, and whether window/tentative-state bookkeeping carries device assumptions that break under a re-keyed second-device graph. None of these is verified by the code reading; each is an audit item in Section 6's first-boot checklist.

One payoff-relevant fact completes the picture, and it must be stated honestly: the placement itself costs almost nothing in step time — verification of six tokens changes the *size* of activations crossing the five stages, not the number of crossings — but **the expert-read tax under verification is large, unmeasured, and wide enough to flip the sign of the whole payoff**. The arithmetic of the tax is solid: verifying a linear block of 6 positions reads ~35 unique experts per layer instead of ~6 (closed form: 384·(1−(1−6/384)⁶) ≈ 35, ~9% of experts), at ~18.6 MB per expert in MXFP4 — ≈4.06 GiB of extra weight reads per step across 8 layers (arithmetic from the MoE configuration). The prior art anticipated exactly this failure mode for MoE speculation [[260216052-moe-spec-expert-budgeting-for-efficient-speculative-decoding]]; DSpark's linear block avoids the tree regime but not the fan-out cost. What is *not* solid is the bandwidth those reads run at. This node's own control measurements [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]] show the card streams a contiguous 1 GiB copy at 1,597 GB/s (99.8% of peak) but an 1,152×5120 GEMM at 11.2 MiB at only 384 GB/s (24%), and 256 separate 64 KiB reads at 4.7 GB/s — and our per-expert work, a 16×5120×2304 GEMV gathered 6-of-384, is *smaller* and more scattered than the 384 GB/s case. The honest window for Marlin MXFP4 expert reads on this silicon is therefore ~150–400 GB/s, and the specific rate is unmeasured. The tax follows: ~11 ms at 384 GB/s (+48% on the ~24 ms step) to ~29 ms at 150 GB/s (+121%). 

The payoff is genuinely two-sided. At τ = 2.0 accepted tokens: ~1.37× at the low tax, ~0.91× at the high — a net loss. At τ = 3.5, which DSpark's own math/code figures (5.12–5.57) support, an 11 ms tax yields ~2.4×. The replicated ~1.4–3× band on this hardware class therefore holds only if the tax lands in the lower half of its range. Two unmeasured quantities gate the payoff, and the acceptance rate is not the more dangerous one: the expert-read rate scales the *entire* verification cost, not just the accepted length. Timing a verification-shaped batch on this node (Section 6's first gate) decides which world this node is in. Reading the outcome also requires the accepted-length-versus-block-size tradeoff of the adaptive-speculation literature [[240519715-specdec-boosting-speculative-decoding-via-adaptive-candidate-lengths]]: DSpark's block is a fixed 5, so the accepted length the workload sustains — not the block size — is what converts the tax arithmetic into a speedup, which is why the acceptance measurement of Section 3.E and the rate measurement above are the two numbers that jointly settle the case.

## 5. Alternatives Ranked, and the Recommended Configuration

The ranking question needs a named decision rule [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]], because three defensible orderings exist depending on whether one optimizes for cost, comfort, or headroom. The rule used here: rank routes by (a) structural fit (code reading + arithmetic; load and graph-capture unconfirmed unless stated), (b) accuracy cost, (c) one-time cost (money, download, code), and (d) independent payoff that does not depend on the unmeasured DSpark acceptance rate. A route that fails (a) is out regardless of (b)–(d).

| REAP-320E, five cards | +0.70 GiB in-budget; ≈−0.9 GiB physical after the wedge | ~+1.5–2.2% ppl (extrapolated; 304E measured +2.21%) | Build checkpoint from public recipe; **zero code change** — 320 is in the expert-count table | None | **Probe — expected to fail its first boot** |
| REAP-272E, five cards | +7.43 GiB slack (8-layer rank 54.65 GiB vs 62.08) | +4.13% held-out ppl (published) | ~208 GiB download; one kernel-table case + `_moe_C` rebuild | None | **Fallback — expected survivor on five cards** |
| Sixth card, PP6 `7,7,7,7,7,5` | +8.36 GiB worst-rank, +14.03 GiB draft rank | **Zero** (full 384E checkpoint) | Hardware + PSU/slot survey + pre-flight gates | **~7× KV pool**; lower load peak (54.35 vs 61.24 GiB) | **Preferred if budget allows** |

**The asymmetry that decides the ranking: pruning buys memory, not speed.** The REAP card states it verbatim: "Active parameters are unchanged (6 routed + 1 shared per token) — pruning buys memory, not decode speed" [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]. This matters more than it first appears. The REAP routes are a *pure* accuracy-for-fit trade: they purchase the ~8 GiB the draft needs by shrinking the target, and deliver nothing else. The sixth card is a fit purchase *plus* an independent payoff — a KV pool that expands roughly 7× (toward the multi-million-token pools the field deployments report), a lower and safer load peak, and headroom for every future feature that would otherwise be stolen from the KV pool. That payoff is accuracy-independent but not unconditional: it is a *concurrency* payoff, and realising it at batch>1 depends on the batch>1 correctness gates of Section 6 being resolved — the BLEED corruption — which is a different gate from the acceptance measurement, and one closable by applying identified fixes rather than by measurement. Until it closes, the sixth card's realized benefit is single-stream context headroom. **No reader should walk away assuming pruning improves decode; it does not.** There is a second-order interaction worth naming: pruning shrinks the target's expert pool and therefore slightly *reduces* the per-step expert-read tax of speculative verification (fewer experts to fan out over), but the effect is marginal and cannot rescue the speedup case for the REAP routes — the speedup comes from the draft's accepted length, which pruning does not touch.

**Route 1 — REAP-320E: cheapest probe, zero serving-side code, thinnest margin.** 320 is present in the fused router's expert-count table in the fork's `csrc/libtorch_stable/moe/topk_softplus_sqrt_kernels.cu` (256, 320, 384 are present; 272 and 288 are absent — fork source; the table membership is also documented in the REAP card [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]), so the checkpoint loads with no kernel patch and no rebuild. The arithmetic from measured per-layer values (a 7.089 GiB layer = 6.724 GiB of experts, which scale with expert count, + 0.365 GiB other) gives an 8-layer rank of 61.38 GiB against the 62.08 budget — +0.70 GiB slack *with the draft included*. But that margin is in-budget accounting, and Section 1 establishes a ~1.6 GiB physical wedge (64,337 of 65,536 MiB observed on the live draft rank): the implied *physical* margin of the 320E rank is roughly −0.9 GiB. Failure at first load is therefore the *expected* branch, not a surprise. The route is still worth attempting first, because it is a cheap zero-code probe — a failed load costs only time — but it is a probe, not a destination; the expected survivor on five cards is 272E, whose +7.43 GiB clears the wedge by ~5.8 GiB. 

Two honest caveats travel with the probe. First, no published 320E checkpoint exists: one must be built from the public recipe, and the calibration tooling cannot run locally on sm_80. The failure is precise: the reference implementation's saliency collector needs TileLang `T.gemm` on FP8 E4M3 tensor cores, and FP8 MMA first appears at sm_89 — sm_80 has none [[cuda-compute-capability-nvidia-gpu-compatibility-table-gpu-ark]] [[nvidia-ampere-architecture-in-depth-nvidia-technical-blog]]. The blockage is narrow, though: all six TileLang kernels compile on sm_80 and the activation-quant path works — only the fp8/fp4 GEMMs fail (`torch._scaled_mm` on fp8 raises; fp8 `a @ b` raises `NotImplementedError`) — and two concrete workarounds exist: a CPU/dequantized reference forward, or a Triton dequant forward built from this fork's sm80 Marlin/Triton kernels (the fork's `fp8_sm80.py` e4m3 encode/decode is confirmed working standalone). Text-only calibration suffices — pass `--allow-text-only`; the tooling's mandatory image-calibration warning does not apply to this text-only deployment. That is real work the *zero-code-change* claim does not cover; what it covers is the *serving* side only. Second, the 61.24 GiB Marlin-repack load peak is the binding moment. The boot itself is the experiment, with the falsifier named in Section 6. Its accuracy price is an extrapolated ~+1.5–2.2% held-out perplexity: 320E sits *above* the published ladder (320 > 304 > 288), so the honest bound is the 304E measurement of +2.21%, with ~+1.5% the optimistic extrapolation [[github-libertaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-reap-expert-pruning-for-deepseek-v41-flas]] — and the general expert-pruning literature reports post-training expert pruning and skipping that "maintain satisfactory performance" across a wide range of tasks while reducing model size [[240214800-not-all-experts-are-equal-efficient-expert-pruning-and-skipping-for-mi]] — a weaker claim than monotonic degradation in expert count, but one that supports the extrapolation without replacing checkpoint-specific measurement.

**Route 2 — REAP-272E: comfortable margin, published artifact, one kernel case.** The pre-pruned checkpoint cuts routed experts 384→272 per layer, 475.2→396.8 GiB, experts untouched at MXFP4 [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]; the same per-layer arithmetic gives an 8-layer rank of 54.65 GiB vs 62.08 — +7.43 GiB slack, comfortable against any accounting wedge. Its prices: the 272 case is missing from the expert-count table, so one added case plus a `_moe_C` rebuild is required (small, verified-shaped work — the table structure is trivially extensible); ~208 GiB of download, partially offset by reusing Engram shards already on disk; and the published +4.13% held-out perplexity regression (+5.49% if one ever dropped to 256E). What it buys over Route 1 is certainty: a published artifact whose perplexity is measured rather than interpolated, and a memory margin that no load-time surprise threatens.

**Route 3 — the sixth card at PP6: widest margin, zero accuracy cost, independent payoff** [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. Every structural gate of Section 4 passes with zero code changes, the configuration class is field-proven on this exact GPU model, and the unmodified 384E checkpoint is kept — no perplexity premium, no calibration pipeline, no kernel rebuild. The costs: hardware (~250 W per card, PSU headroom, a free slot and riser on this specific board — node-local facts a physical survey must settle), and the unmeasured six-hop latency tax. What it uniquely buys: a ~7× KV pool, the lower load peak, ~8 GiB of steady-state headroom, and the DSpark speedup option on the same hardware — bought once, delivered regardless of what the acceptance measurement returns (the KV pool's value at batch>1 still rides on the BLEED gate of Section 6, a correctness fix rather than a measurement).

**The migration closure constrains all of the above, so it is stated once.** Upstream vLLM cannot serve this checkpoint on this node at all: it lacks the fork's shadow-source machinery, without which no legal PP split exists for 64 GiB ranks (the group-aligned split forces a 20-layer rank at 147.8 GB that must offload experts, collapsing decode), and upstream has zero verified Ampere profiles for DSpark — an audited absence with stated scope: direct inspection of the upstream recipes documentation lists verified profiles only for RTX PRO 6000 (sm_120), ROCm MI300X, H200 and DGX Spark, and a targeted search for any Ampere/sm_80 row found none [[deepseek-aideepseek-v4-flash-vllm-recipes]] — that absence is itself evidence for the closure, since upstream documents no supported path for this silicon; and direct inspection of the fork's Releases page found it empty (Docker-tags-only), so no changelog contradicts this. 

Even if the disaggregation RFC merged tomorrow, the draft's coupling (hidden states, shared head, ring windows) blocks it. Every route here therefore executes *within this fork*, and cherry-picking upstream fixes forward is the correct direction of travel. Should the direction ever reverse, the porting inventory is short and known: shadow-source plumbing (three files, plus `produce_kv_side_effects` and the shadow-feed buffers in the load path), the staged Marlin repack, the exact-size `cudaHostAlloc` Engram allocator, and the fork's DSpark `spec_decode` package — nothing else in the fork is unique — with RFC #53673 (draft parallel-config decoupling) and PR #44698 (MTP-under-PP correctness) as the identified cherry-pick-forward candidates. There is no software escape hatch [[github-wtdcodevllm-backport-dogfooding-vllm-backport-for-older-gpus-github]].

**Recommendation.** By the decision rule [[libertaidaideepseek-v41-flash-reap-272e-hugging-face]]: the sixth card at PP6 is preferred *if the KV pool matters and the budget allows* — it is the only zero-accuracy-cost route and the only route with an independent payoff (accuracy-independent, though concurrency-gated on the BLEED fix above), and buying it buys the DSpark option on the same hardware; buying neither buys neither. If no budget exists, the order of attack is: attempt the 320E probe first — cheapest on the serving side, no kernel work — expecting its boot to fail on the wedge arithmetic, and promote 272E from fallback to primary when it does; its +7.43 GiB margin is comfortable against any accounting wedge. 

What would change the answer [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]]: a measured Marlin MXFP4 expert-read rate in the low hundreds of GB/s removes the speedup case at moderate accepted lengths — this is the more dangerous of the two gates, since it scales the entire verification cost rather than just the accepted length — and a measured DSpark acceptance below ~1.2 accepted tokens on reasoning traces removes it independently; the KV-pool case for the card survives both. A fork revision tightening the aux assertion beyond `last_start ≤ 36` would close the PP6 route and shift weight to the REAP paths.

## 6. Prerequisites, Risks, and Verification Plan

The single highest-value next action costs nothing, and it comes first because it decides whether the speculative-decoding case exists at all: **time a verification-shaped batch on this node — six tokens per request, top-6 routing — and read the Marlin MXFP4 expert-read rate directly.** It costs an afternoon, not a card. Section 4 shows the payoff is two-sided on this single number: a rate near 400 GB/s keeps the speedup positive at τ = 2.0; a rate near 150 GB/s makes it a net loss regardless of what the acceptance measurement returns. Next comes the acceptance measurement: **DSpark's accepted length on reasoning-dominated prompts at this node's concurrency** — a number that exists nowhere in the record. The evidence standing in for it is the reconciled pair of Section 3.E — a generic-drafter study cautioning that reasoning is where drafters fail [[260414682-acceptance-dynamics-across-cognitive-domains-in-speculative-decoding]], and DSpark's own trained-drafter data showing reasoning is where it succeeds (accepted length 5.57 on math, 5.12 on code, versus 3.49 on chat; post-pruning acceptance above 92% on all structured tasks) [[dspark-confidence-scheduled-speculative-decoding-with-semi-autoregressive-genera]] — but neither number was measured on this model, this draft, or this workload, and the vendor's own deployment doc concedes the gap: "Quality and performance acceptance remain incomplete" [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]], with numerical equivalence, calibrated scheduling and throughput still requiring validation for the deployment workload. 

The measurement protocol: run the target against a fixed reasoning-heavy prompt suite with DSpark enabled, read accepted length directly from vLLM's spec-decode metrics (the config surface where the method and its metrics are selected [[vllmvllmconfigspeculativepy-at-main-vllm-projectvllm-github]]), and require aggregate statistics — because DSpark is nondeterministic [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]] ("single decode numbers are worth about ±10%," with one operator watching prose swing 71.9 → 137.7 tok/s on an unchanged server [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]). The thresholds: accepted length **≥ ~2** on reasoning traces puts the node in the replicated mid-band, *conditional on the expert-read tax landing in the lower half of its range* (Section 4); **≥ ~3.5** approaches the paper's structured-task figures and the ~2.4× upper case; below **~1.4×** falls under the break-even line — which itself moves with the measured tax — and kills the speedup case, leaving the sixth card to stand on the KV pool alone. The regime literature is more favourable than the folk claim: speculative decoding is classically understood to pay only at small batch, but MagicDec shows that with a sufficiently sparse draft KV cache and moderate-to-long sequences the speedup persists even in the high-throughput regime — "an intelligent drafting strategy can achieve better speedup with increasing batch size" [[240811049-magicdec-breaking-the-latency-throughput-tradeoff-for-long-context-gen]]. The single-stream posture for the acceptance measurement stands on this report's own grounds (aggregate-free single-decode numbers are unreliable, per the nondeterminism above), not on any claim that speculation collapses at concurrency — and MagicDec's regime is, if anything, the one a multi-million-token sixth-card pool would operate in. A published V4.1-family acceptance figure of ≥ 0.8 per position on reasoning would substitute for local measurement; none exists, which is why the local gate stands.

### A. Pre-flight correctness gates — mandatory before any route

All documented on sm_80 hardware — the PR's test rig is 8× A800 80GB, the same GPU class as this node's CMP 170HX parts, though not the same card [[feature-sm8x-ampere-a100a800-support-for-deepseek-v4-flash-deepseek-v4-flash-073]]. These are correctness items, not tuning, and a corrupted baseline makes the acceptance measurement of the previous paragraph meaningless — which is why they precede it in execution order even though the measurement costs nothing.

1. **Patch or gate `persistent_topk`.** It has no SM90 capability gate and returns wrong indices when the candidate count falls between k and 2k — for `index_topk=512` and `compress_ratio=4`, that is prompt lengths 2049–4096, a very ordinary range. It does not crash; it "emits fluent-looking degenerate text, so it's easy to miss." Verify with needle-retrieval probes at 2k–4.5k prompt lengths before and after any change [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]].
2. **Apply the top-k tie-handling fix `9b82ec4cba`.** This commit alone took a comparable rig from 15/20 to 20/20 correct at 67k tokens; the moe_align fix (`f02954b96`) alone did not [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. Make the 20/20 needle suite the acceptance test for every subsequent change to the stack.
3. **Hold `--max-num-seqs 1` until batch>1 corruption is resolved.** Single-stream is clean (1,000 requests across 20 lengths, 100% correct); a concurrency probe found BLEED — a request returning another request's passphrase. Any batch>1 deployment is gated on reproducing and fixing this; until then, aggregate measurements must be single-stream [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. Resolving BLEED is also the gate that unlocks the sixth card's KV-pool justification (Section 5) — a correctness fix, not a measurement.
4. **Test `expandable_segments` explicitly; do not assume either report.** One rig hard-fails at load ("cannot map 20 MB with 28.6 GiB free — CUDA VMM appears broken on GA100 CMP parts"); this node's production launcher sets the flag and works. Driver-dependent — one reboot with it off is the experiment, and the result should be recorded before any route changes the driver or launcher.
5. **Keep the indexer logits-transient cap.** The long-context fault is the indexer's `[M,N]` logits transient (Xid 31 MMU fault above ~134k tokens); the row-chunk dial restores million-token ceilings [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. Confirm the launcher's setting survives into any new launch script unchanged.
6. **Re-partition for the KV pool, never as a cure.** The `12,12,12,7` field rebalance grew the pool 85% while "the ceiling did not move by one token" [[vllm-50576-thread-comments-cmp-170hx-dspark-field-evidence-deployments-throughpu]]. Rebalancing buys capacity, not context-length repair; nobody should expect the PP6 move to fix a long-context bug.

### B. Per-route validation and falsifiers

**For PP6**, six validations, in order:

- Boot with `VLLM_PP_LAYER_PARTITION=7,7,7,7,7,5` explicitly pinned.
- Confirm the last-stage Marlin-repack peak (predicted 40.56 GiB) and every other rank stay under budget during load.
- Measure six-hop step latency against the PP5 baseline — a step above ~1.4× the five-hop baseline would make PP6 net-negative for latency despite installing.
- Verify drafter CUDA-graph capture at six-rank PP, which has no measured precedent (capture failure is a blocker, with a clean fallback: the REAP routes on five cards remain intact).
- Audit the three open draft-side questions of Section 4: draft-device embedding replication for the noise-token/Markov path, Engram-history access from the draft rank, and hc-widened-residual engagement and window bookkeeping under a re-keyed graph.
- Confirm the ~2.61 GiB graph-pool reserve measured at PP5 transfers, since the draft rank's +14.03 GiB slack absorbs it either way. **For 320E:** the load *is* the falsifier — the 8-layer rank's repack peak (predicted 61.24 GiB against the 62.08 budget) must be observed to complete, and post-load free memory must clear the ~1.6 GiB context/NCCL/graph wedge that in-budget arithmetic does not see; if either fails — the expected branch, given the wedge — 272E is the promoted survivor within the same pipeline. **For 272E:** validate the added kernel case against all batch shapes in the correctness suite before serving traffic. In all cases, validate lifecycle correctness before throughput: the only published DSV4.1+DSpark configuration is TP4 at `max_model_len=4096` [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]], so a 1M-context PP deployment is unvalidated on three axes at once (architecture, topology, workload), and the first milestone is a lifecycle soak, not a benchmark.

### C. Standing risks, honestly held

The six-hop latency tax is unmeasured and bounds the payoff of the recommended route; the working prior is a ~20% decode-latency rise before spec-decode amortizes it. 

A scope risk sits underneath everything: DSpark's documented supported deployment scope is TP4 with whole-expert EP, BF16 KV and compilation level 0 at `max_model_len=4096` [[deepseek_v41_dsparkmd]] — this deployment is a port of an explicitly TP4-scoped implementation to PP5 on sm_80, a port ranked co-equal with the acceptance measurement among the go/no-go risks. The expert-read tax of Section 4 is unmeasured and two-sided. The ±10% decode nondeterminism means every before/after claim in this plan must rest on aggregates over many requests. Re-partitioning does not fix long-context ceilings — the KV-pool payoff of the sixth card is a concurrency payoff, not a context-repair one. And the favourable acceptance prior rests on other model families: if the shipped V4.1-Flash draft behaves on reasoning traces the way the generic-drafter study predicts rather than the way DSpark's own data predicts, the speedup collapses to break-even and only the KV-pool case survives.

### D. What is settled, and what is not

Settled by measurement and arithmetic:

- nothing on five unmodified cards fits;
- every squeeze lever is closed by a number, not an opinion;
- all three surviving routes install structurally (code reading + arithmetic — load and graph-capture unconfirmed);
- the configuration class is field-proven on this exact GPU.

Not settled:

- the Marlin expert-read rate that decides the sign of the verification tax [[exl3-moe-decode-shape-profiling-on-cmp-170hx-sm_80-small-gemm-bandwidth-ceiling]];
- the acceptance rate that decides whether the fit pays;
- the six-hop latency that decides how much of the headroom converts to throughput;
- whether the 320E margin survives its first boot. The architecture of the decision generalizes beyond this node — for any model whose draft consumes late-layer hidden states, an uneven partition that lightens the final stage is the natural fit rather than a compromise, and the memory problem and the payoff problem are separate purchases that must be evaluated separately. Here, the sixth card converts a stack at −0.02 GiB of slack — where every future feature must be stolen from the KV pool — into one with ~8 GiB of headroom, a ~7× context ceiling, and a speculative-decoding option whose expected value the evidence now leans in favour of, and whose actual value becomes known the moment the free measurement is run. **Run the measurement, pass the gates, then buy.**
